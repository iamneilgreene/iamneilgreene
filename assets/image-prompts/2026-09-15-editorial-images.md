## Image 1: Ideas / The working page

### Exact production prompt

Create one highly realistic, quietly distinctive editorial still-life photograph for the Ideas section of a personal capability and leadership website. Landscape 3:2 composition. This is a deliberately constructed editorial concept about thinking and writing, not documentation of an actual author workspace. The emotional register is calm, exact, practical, and earned.

SUBJECT AND MATERIALS: On a modest, well-made dark oak table, show one open, unbranded warm-bone notebook with unruled pages carrying only a few faint, irregular, nonlinguistic graphite strokes and a small crossed-out geometric sketch, with no letters, numbers, or readable writing and one graphite mechanical pencil, with a plain closed charcoal book partially visible behind the notebook. The notebook has a thin, dark charcoal cloth cover, believable stitched binding, a slight natural curve near its spine, and uncoated paper with subtle fibers. The pencil lies on the tabletop beside the outer page edge, not floating over it. These are the only three objects. The wood grain is straight, understated, and matte, with very light everyday wear. This is useful working furniture, neither rustic farmhouse styling nor conspicuous luxury. No person, hand, portrait, or reflection of a person appears.

COMPOSITION: An intimate, oblique close-up from approximately 45 degrees above the tabletop. Turn the notebook slightly so its spine recedes diagonally into the frame. The open pages are the dominant visual form; the pencil is a small secondary line. Place the complete notebook and pencil inside the central 65 percent of the image, with both objects fully visible and at least 10 percent quiet margin around their outer edges. Keep the meaningful arrangement readable in a centered square crop. Preserve asymmetry and a sense of an actual working surface rather than a perfectly aligned product catalog display. Do not use a top-down flat lay. There is no baked-in text or space intended for a text overlay; the website provides the words alongside the image.

CAMERA AND LIGHT: The photographic character of a full-frame camera with a 50 mm lens at f/4, natural perspective, fine material detail, no exaggerated wide-angle stretching. Focus cleanly on the near notebook page and pencil point; let the far edge of the closed book soften gradually. Let the farther table surface soften gently through distance. One broad window outside the frame on camera left supplies soft morning daylight. Light reveals the paper's subtle texture and the wood grain without shiny glare. Natural contact shadows anchor every object. Gentle reflected room light keeps the graphite pencil and dark cover separable from the tabletop at small website sizes. No visible lighting equipment, spotlight, sunbeam, or theatrical haze.

GRADE AND FINISH: Warm bone highlights and restrained brown oak against navy-tinted ink shadows. Neutral-warm paper, not yellow parchment. Muted saturation, moderate contrast, soft highlight rolloff, visible shadow detail, subtle photographic grain, and believable microscopic imperfections. The frame should pair naturally with a navy, bone, graphite, and restrained-bronze editorial website. No pure-black crushed areas or stark white clipping. Natural image texture, not the waxy polished finish of a 3D render.

NEGATIVES AND EXCLUSIONS: No readable writing, handwriting, linguistic symbols, lettering, numbers, logos, watermark, book title, fake manuscript, screen, decorative plant, cup, spectacles, luxury watch, coins, compass, chess pieces, weapons, motivational symbolism, bright gold, neon, teal-and-orange grading, excessive bokeh, heavy vignette, artificial bloom, CGI sheen, duplicate pencils, impossible notebook binding, melted page edges, floating objects, or people.

## Image 2: Community / Room for conversation

### Exact production prompt

Create one highly realistic editorial environment photograph for a community pilot section on a capability and leadership website. Landscape 3:2 composition. This is an imagined, deliberately unoccupied space that illustrates the possibility of conversation. It must not imply that a real meeting has already happened or that this is an actual booked venue. The emotional register is welcoming, grounded, calm, and quietly purposeful.

SUBJECT AND ROOM: A modest round dark-oak table surrounded by exactly five simple, well-made chairs in a small, ordinary, thoughtfully maintained room. The chairs have slim dark wood frames and muted graphite fabric seats; their construction is physically plausible and consistent. All five chairs are empty. Two plain unbranded bone-paper notebooks and two clear water glasses sit casually on the tabletop, with no other props. Their arrangement is loose and useful, not ceremonially symmetrical. Reflections and water levels are physically plausible. Show a matte, warm-bone plaster wall and a plain tall window to the right, with no recognizable building or external landmark. A dark, subdued floor provides a grounded base. The room feels attainable and human, like a useful local meeting space, not a private members club, hotel boardroom, luxury dining room, therapy office, or corporate training center. No person, silhouette, reflection, portrait, event branding, or historical trace of a gathering appears.

COMPOSITION: Photograph from an unobtrusive position just outside the conversational grouping, at a seated eye height of approximately 1.2 meters, with a slight downward angle that makes the clear tabletop visible. The round table sits near the middle of the frame. Five chairs are naturally spaced around it, with the nearest one offset enough to offer a clear visual opening into the group. Keep the whole tabletop and recognizable chair backs within the central 70 percent of the image; keep chair legs physically grounded in the wider frame. Avoid tangent collisions between chair backs and table edges. Preserve a centered-square crop in which the table and the circle-of-conversation idea remain clear. Leave quiet architectural breathing room around the grouping. The image should be intimate enough to suggest repeated conversations, not a grand room awaiting a crowd. Do not add overlay text.

CAMERA AND LIGHT: Full-frame architectural editorial photography with a 35 mm lens at approximately f/5.6. Natural perspective, straight verticals, no stretched furniture or exaggerated wide-angle corners. The table and chair grouping remain in convincing focus; the far wall may soften very slightly through distance. A broad window on camera right supplies soft, indirect daylight with the same calm natural quality as the companion notebook image. Keep the actual window glass subdued rather than blown out. Gentle room bounce lights the dark furniture so each chair remains distinct. Soft, plausible contact shadows connect every leg to the floor. No stage lights, dramatic shaft of light, studio haze, artificial rim light, or theatrical darkness.

GRADE AND FINISH: Navy-inflected ink shadows, graphite upholstery, restrained dark brown oak, warm bone walls, and natural neutral daylight. Desaturated and composed, with readable midtones and subtle material texture. Bone is warm-neutral rather than creamy yellow; dark furniture retains its grain and edge definition. No heavy contrast, black crush, glossy luxury treatment, or glowing blue cast. Retain a little realistic photographic grain and everyday imperfection, without clutter or staged distress. The scene should harmonize with a calm ink-and-bone editorial brand while feeling approachable.

NEGATIVES AND EXCLUSIONS: No people, faces, hands, human reflections, audience, crowd, stage, podium, event banner, venue name, company logo, watermark, text, motivational poster, additional books, additional props, cups, food, alcohol, candles, trophies, flags, secret-society symbols, compass, shields, chess pieces, bright gold, luxury leather club chairs, hotel decor, boardroom screen, neon lighting, teal-and-orange grade, extreme bokeh, fisheye distortion, impossible furniture joints, extra chair legs, duplicated chairs, intersecting furniture, floating objects, or documentary-event styling.

## Output review gate

Evaluate the actual generated images, not merely these prompts. Inspect at native size and at approximately 320 px wide, and preview both 3:2 and centered-square crops.

1. Intent and placement: each image supports its adjacent section without adding an unsupported factual claim.
2. Subject integrity: notebook binding, pencil geometry, table construction, chair count, joins, legs, and contact shadows are plausible.
3. Crop resilience: the central subject remains comprehensible with no critical truncation.
4. Brand continuity: both share quiet natural daylight, ink shadows, bone highlights, and restrained natural materials.
5. Readability: dark furniture and small objects do not disappear at mobile size.
6. Restraint: no accidental text, logos, generic filler, or invented event evidence.
7. Page hierarchy: the images support existing content and do not compete with Neil's hero portrait.

Use each standing council’s saved weighted model when numerical scoring is requested; report each criterion and total out of 100. Any misleading evidence, identity substitution, or major physical artifact blocks acceptance regardless of the mean. Revise only the failing attributes while preserving accepted art direction.
